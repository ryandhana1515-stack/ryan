FUSIONTECH AI
MASTER CEO BRAIN + SME OPERATING SYSTEM + CLIENT ONBOARDING
+ CRM/ERP + WEBSITE AGENT BUILD DIRECTIVE

IMPORTANT:
Read this entire instruction before making changes.

You are operating as the Lead AI Systems Architect, Product Architect,
Automation Engineer, Knowledge Architect and Full-Stack Engineering Agent
for FusionTech AI.

This is not a brainstorming exercise.

Do not simply explain what could be built.

Inspect the work that already exists in this project and improve the
existing architecture rather than unnecessarily rebuilding working systems.

Our objective is to create a commercially deployable AI Company Operating
System that FusionTech can customize and sell to SMEs and professional
businesses.

## PART 1 — FUSIONTECH'S CORE PRODUCT

FusionTech is building and selling:

THE CEO BRAIN + AI WORKFORCE + CRM / ERP / COMPANY DATA LAYER + BUSINESS AUTOMATION + CUSTOM SOFTWARE + HIGH-END WEBSITES

The CEO Brain should become an intelligence and management layer sitting
above a customer's company.

It should connect, where authorized, to the customer's:

CRM, ERP, WhatsApp, Email, Calendar, Website, Lead forms, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Advertising, Sales pipeline, Customer service, Projects, Documents, Cloud storage, Accounting system, Invoices, Payments, Employees, Tasks, SOPs, Company knowledge, Marketing, Operations, Appointments, Other business software

The system must not assume every customer uses the same software.

The architecture must be modular.

## PART 2 — THE CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE

The customer should NOT experience FusionTech like a traditional IT
consultant asking: "Send us all your company data."

Most SME owners will not understand that request. They may not know: where their data is, how to export it, what a CRM is, what an API is, what information FusionTech needs, what files matter, what systems can be connected.

Therefore build the experience around: THE FUSIONTECH COMPANY DISCOVERY AGENT

The customer's experience should feel like talking to a highly experienced
business consultant. The agent should progressively understand the company.

## PART 3 — COMPANY DISCOVERY AGENT

Create/design an agent called: COMPANY DISCOVERY & ONBOARDING AGENT

Its mission is to convert an ordinary conversation with a business owner
into a structured digital model of their company.

Do NOT ask 100 questions at once. Have a natural conversation. Start with simple questions.

Example: "Tell me about your company. What do you sell and approximately how many people work there?"

Then progressively discover:

COMPANY — Company name, Brand name, Industry, Locations, Markets, Products, Services, Number of employees, Departments, Management structure

CUSTOMERS — Who buys from them? B2B or B2C? Typical customer, Customer segments, Customer journey, Repeat customers, Existing customer database

SALES — Where do leads come from? Who receives leads? How quickly are leads answered? Who follows up? How many follow-ups occur? How are quotations created? How are deals tracked? Where are leads lost? What defines a qualified lead? What defines a hot lead?

MARKETING — Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Google, YouTube, Website, SEO, Email marketing, Advertising agencies, Content production, Lead forms

COMMUNICATION — WhatsApp, WhatsApp Business, Email, Phone, SMS, Website chat, Social DMs

SOFTWARE — CRM, ERP, Accounting, POS, Booking system, Spreadsheets, Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Cloud storage, Industry-specific software, Internal applications

OPERATIONS — How does work enter the company? Who performs it? How are tasks assigned? How are deadlines tracked? What requires approval? Where do delays happen?

CUSTOMER SERVICE — How customers ask for help, Who responds, Common questions, Complaints, Refund processes, Escalations, Support channels

FINANCE — Accounting software, Invoices, Quotations, Payment collection, Expense processes, Outstanding payment follow-up. Do not automatically request sensitive financial records unless genuinely required for the approved implementation.

HR / TEAM — Departments, Roles, Approval hierarchy, Task allocation, Internal communications, Employee onboarding, SOPs

MANAGEMENT — What does the CEO currently check every day? What reports does the CEO receive? What information is difficult to obtain? What decisions take too long? What problems repeatedly surprise management?

AUTOMATION — Ask: "What work does your team repeat every day?" "What do employees constantly copy and paste?" "What gets forgotten?" "What takes too long?" "What do customers repeatedly ask?" "What does management wish happened automatically?" These answers reveal automation opportunities.

## PART 4 — DO NOT ASK THE CUSTOMER TO 'GIVE US ALL YOUR DATA'

This is extremely important. FusionTech must make data onboarding EASY. The Discovery Agent should identify WHERE information currently exists. Then create an onboarding plan.

- CUSTOMER SAYS: "Our customers are in Excel." SYSTEM RESPONDS: "That's fine. We can help you import the spreadsheet. You don't need to organize everything manually first."
- CUSTOMER SAYS: "Everything is in WhatsApp." SYSTEM RESPONDS: "Understood. We'll first determine which WhatsApp Business setup you're using and what information can be connected or migrated appropriately."
- CUSTOMER SAYS: "We use HubSpot." SYSTEM: Recommend authorized HubSpot integration.
- CUSTOMER SAYS: "We use Google Drive." SYSTEM: Request appropriate Google Drive authorization and identify relevant folders/documents.
- CUSTOMER SAYS: "I don't know where anything is." SYSTEM: Run a guided Data Discovery Checklist.

## PART 5 — DATA DISCOVERY CHECKLIST

The system should progressively identify: Customer records, Lead records, Sales records, Product/service information, Pricing, FAQs, SOPs, Documents, Templates, Policies, Past quotations, Contracts, Marketing materials, Website content, Email information, Calendars, Project information, Operational spreadsheets, Accounting platform, Support information, Employee roles.

For every category classify: AVAILABLE / NOT AVAILABLE / UNKNOWN / CONNECTION REQUIRED / IMPORT REQUIRED / NOT REQUIRED.

Also record: SOURCE SYSTEM, OWNER, FORMAT, APPROXIMATE VOLUME, SENSITIVITY, IMPORT METHOD, AUTHORIZATION REQUIRED.

## PART 6 — CONNECT, DON'T COPY EVERYTHING

The CEO Brain should not blindly duplicate every piece of customer data. Determine whether information should be:

- CONNECTED LIVE — Example: CRM, Calendar, Accounting, Current sales pipeline
- IMPORTED — Example: Existing customer spreadsheet
- INDEXED / RETRIEVED WHEN NEEDED — Example: SOPs, Policies, Knowledge documents
- SUMMARIZED INTO KNOWLEDGE — Example: Company procedures
- LEFT IN THE ORIGINAL SYSTEM — when moving it provides no benefit.

Build connectors around authorized APIs and integrations whenever practical.

## PART 7 — CLIENT DIGITAL COMPANY MAP

After discovery, automatically generate: CLIENT DIGITAL COMPANY MAP. It should contain: COMPANY PROFILE, DEPARTMENTS, PEOPLE / ROLES, CURRENT SOFTWARE, DATA SOURCES, COMMUNICATION CHANNELS, LEAD SOURCES, SALES PROCESS, CUSTOMER JOURNEY, OPERATIONS PROCESS, FINANCE PROCESS, MARKETING PROCESS, APPROVAL STRUCTURE, PAIN POINTS, AUTOMATION OPPORTUNITIES, INTEGRATION REQUIREMENTS, SECURITY REQUIREMENTS, CEO REPORTING REQUIREMENTS.

This becomes the blueprint for that customer's CEO Brain.

## PART 8 — CEO BRAIN GENERATION

After discovery, generate a customized CEO Brain architecture. Do NOT create exactly the same system for every customer. Determine which modules they actually require.

Potential modules: CEO DASHBOARD, SALES BRAIN, MARKETING BRAIN, CUSTOMER SERVICE BRAIN, OPERATIONS BRAIN, PROJECT BRAIN, FINANCE INFORMATION BRAIN, ADMIN BRAIN, KNOWLEDGE BRAIN, REPORTING BRAIN.

## PART 9 — CRM

FusionTech needs a modular CRM suitable for SMEs. Core entities should include: COMPANIES, CONTACTS, LEADS, OPPORTUNITIES, DEALS, PIPELINES, CONVERSATIONS, MESSAGES, TASKS, APPOINTMENTS, QUOTATIONS, PROJECTS, ACTIVITIES, DOCUMENTS, AGENT RUNS, WORKFLOW RUNS, AUDIT LOGS.

Support tenant isolation. Every customer organization must be isolated. Company A must NEVER see Company B's information.

## PART 10 — ERP / OPERATIONS LAYER

Do not attempt to build a universal replacement for every ERP immediately. Create a modular operational architecture. Potential modules: Orders, Projects, Tasks, Inventory where required, Suppliers, Purchasing, Service delivery, Documents, Approvals, Invoices, Payments/status, Employees/roles, Operational reporting.

If a customer already has a suitable ERP, prefer integration rather than forcing replacement.

## PART 11 — AI WORKFORCE

Design the architecture for specialized agents.

1. COMPANY DISCOVERY AGENT — Understands the customer's business.
2. SALES AGENT — Captures, Qualifies, Follows up, Updates CRM, Books appointments, Escalates opportunities.
3. SOLUTION ARCHITECT AGENT — Converts discovery into technical architecture.
4. WEBSITE ARCHITECT AGENT — Determines website requirements.
5. SME WEBSITE BUILDER AGENT — Builds premium SME websites.
6. MEDICAL / DOCTOR WEBSITE BUILDER AGENT — Builds premium healthcare websites with stricter content, privacy and compliance awareness.
7. CUSTOMER SERVICE AGENT — Handles approved support workflows.
8. EMAIL / ADMIN AGENT — Handles administrative workflows.
9. MARKETING AGENT — Coordinates campaigns and content.
10. OPERATIONS AGENT — Tracks work and bottlenecks.
11. FINANCE ASSISTANT — Handles permitted financial information workflows.
12. CEO INTELLIGENCE AGENT — Produces executive intelligence.

## PART 12 — WEBSITE AGENT: CRITICAL UPGRADE

A website agent already exists in this project. DO NOT blindly create another duplicate agent. Inspect the existing website agent first. Upgrade/refactor it into a premium website production system.

We require TWO SPECIALIZED MODES: A. SME WEBSITE AGENT, B. DOCTOR / MEDICAL WEBSITE AGENT. They may share common infrastructure but must have separate: Discovery logic, Design intelligence, Page requirements, Content rules, Industry considerations, Quality assurance.

## PART 13 — SME WEBSITE AGENT

The SME Website Agent must be capable of producing premium sites for: Professional services, Beauty, Property, Technology, Consulting, Retail, Education, Home services, B2B, Local businesses, Other SMEs.

Before designing, understand: Business, Audience, Offer, Brand, Competitors, Desired action, Existing website, Existing assets, Style preference, Market positioning.

## PART 14 — MEDICAL / DOCTOR WEBSITE AGENT

Create a specialized medical/doctor website workflow. Possible customers: Doctors, Clinics, Specialists, Dental clinics, Aesthetic practices, Medical groups, Healthcare providers.

It should understand requirements such as: Doctor profiles, Credentials, Specialties, Treatments/services, Clinic locations, Appointments, Contact information, Patient information, FAQs, Educational content, Privacy, Appropriate medical disclaimers, Jurisdiction-specific advertising/compliance review.

Do not fabricate: Doctor qualifications, Medical claims, Success rates, Testimonials, Certifications, Treatment outcomes. Missing medical information must be marked for customer verification.

## PART 15 — $10,000 WEBSITE QUALITY STANDARD

This is CRITICAL. I DO NOT WANT GENERIC AI WEBSITES.

Reject the typical AI design style: dark navy background, purple/blue gradient, random glowing balls, generic glass cards, random stock image, huge generic headline, three feature boxes, generic SaaS layout. Do not treat this as premium design.

Every mock-up should look as though a professional: Brand strategist, UX designer, UI designer, Copywriter, Art director, Motion designer, Front-end engineer worked together.

Before designing, analyze the business category and choose an appropriate visual direction.

## PART 16 — DESIGN INTELLIGENCE

For each website determine:

BRAND PERSONALITY — Luxury? Clinical? Professional? Friendly? Technical? Minimal? Premium? Warm? Corporate? Editorial? Modern?

TYPOGRAPHY — Choose typography appropriate to the brand. Do not use the same font pairing on every website.

LAYOUT — Use intentional hierarchy. Avoid repetitive AI-generated section patterns.

IMAGERY — Use imagery appropriate to the actual business. Do not insert irrelevant stock photography. When custom visual assets would materially improve the design, use the available creative pipeline/tools.

MOTION — Use motion only where it improves experience. Examples: Scroll interactions, Micro-interactions, Transitions, Product/service reveals, Data visualization, Before/after interactions where appropriate, Premium hero animation. Do not overload pages with unnecessary animation.

## PART 17 — AVAILABLE CREATIVE/BUILD TOOLS

Inspect which tools/integrations are genuinely available in the current environment before using them. Potential tools already used by FusionTech include: Lovable, Claude / Claude Code, ChatGPT, GitHub, Higgsfield, Kling, Other approved creative tools. Do NOT pretend a tool is connected if it is not. Use each tool for the job it performs best.

Example architecture: Claude → requirements / architecture / copy / code planning. Lovable → application / website generation where appropriate. Creative visual tools → custom visual assets where appropriate. GitHub → source/version control. Deployment platform → staging/production.

## PART 18 — WEBSITE MOCK-UP SYSTEM

For qualified FusionTech leads, generate a premium initial concept.

Workflow: CLIENT DISCOVERY ↓ INDUSTRY ANALYSIS ↓ BRAND DIRECTION ↓ WEBSITE ARCHITECTURE ↓ COPY DIRECTION ↓ VISUAL DIRECTION ↓ LOVABLE BUILD BRIEF ↓ MOCK-UP ↓ QUALITY REVIEW ↓ HUMAN REVIEW ↓ CUSTOMER PREVIEW

The mock-up must be personalized enough that the customer thinks: "They understand my company." Not: "This is another AI template."

## PART 19 — WEBSITE QA AGENT

Create a quality-control stage. Before any website is shown to a customer, inspect: Desktop responsiveness, Mobile responsiveness, Navigation, Spacing, Typography, Contrast, Accessibility basics, Broken links, Forms, CTAs, Image quality, Image relevance, Copy accuracy, Loading/performance concerns, SEO fundamentals, Metadata, Contact information, Brand consistency, Visual hierarchy, Animations, Claims, Placeholder content.

Medical sites require an additional medical-content verification stage.

## PART 23 — SECURITY

Never ask customers to paste passwords into ordinary AI chats. Use OAuth where possible. Use secure credentials. Use environment variables. Use secret management. Use least privilege. Separate every customer's data. Log important automated actions.

Never expose: API keys, Passwords, Tokens, Private keys, Confidential information.

## PART 24 — HUMAN APPROVAL

AI must not autonomously perform high-risk actions such as: Moving money, Refunding money, Signing contracts, Final pricing commitments, Deleting critical data, Changing critical permissions, Production deployment without authorization, Sending sensitive legal commitments.

Use: AI recommendation ↓ Human approval ↓ Execution ↓ Audit log

## PART 25 — OBSIDIAN / COMPANY KNOWLEDGE

FusionTech uses Obsidian as part of its company knowledge architecture. Organize durable knowledge such as: Company strategy, Products, Services, Sales methodology, SOPs, Agent definitions, Workflow standards, Technical standards, Customer onboarding methodology, Design standards, Decisions, Lessons learned.

Do not use Obsidian as a substitute for the live CRM/database.

LIVE OPERATIONAL DATA: CRM/database. LONG-TERM COMPANY KNOWLEDGE: Obsidian/knowledge system. ORCHESTRATION: n8n. REASONING: AI models.
