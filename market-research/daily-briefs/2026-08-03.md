## 3. Company deep-dive: Nvidia (NVDA) — deep-dive #1

**What it does.** Nvidia designs (but does not manufacture — TSMC does
that) the GPUs that train and run almost every major AI model, plus the
CUDA software layer that keeps developers locked into its chips, and
increasingly whole pre-built server racks.

**How it makes money.** Overwhelmingly by selling data-center accelerators
to a small group of huge customers — the hyperscalers (Microsoft, Amazon,
Google, Meta) and AI labs. In its most recent quarter (Q1 FY2027, ended
26 April 2026), **$75.2B of its $81.6B total revenue came from Data
Center** — about 92% of the business
([Nvidia newsroom](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-first-quarter-fiscal-2027)).

**Revenue trend.** Steeply up. Q1 FY2027 revenue of $81.6B was +20% from
the prior quarter and +85% from a year earlier; the quarter before that
(Q4 FY2026) was $68.1B, +73% year on year
([Nvidia newsroom](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-2026)).
Full fiscal-year 2026 revenue was **$215.9B, up 65%**.

**Profitable?** Extremely — roughly **$120B of net income** on that
$215.9B of FY2026 revenue, a net margin most companies never approach
([ServeTheHome](https://www.servethehome.com/nvidia-reports-q4-fy2026-earnings-data-center-and-proviz-drive-revenue-records/)).

**Main risk.** Customer concentration cuts both ways: a handful of
hyperscalers fund most of that revenue, several are designing their own
chips (see AVGO/MRVL on the watchlist), and July's $1T+ chip selloff showed
what happens to the stock when the market doubts those customers' spending
will pay off
([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/chip-selloff-sk-hynix-samsung-softbank.html)).
Add export-control risk on China sales and the fact that supreme margins
invite competition. Established business, real profits — but priced for
continued extraordinary growth.

## 4. Concept of the day: Market capitalization

**Market cap = share price × number of shares.** It's what the whole
company is valued at by the market, not what one share costs.

Why it matters: a $500 stock is not "more expensive" than a $50 stock in
any meaningful sense. A company with a $50 share price and 10 billion
shares ($500B market cap) is valued at ten times more than one with a $500
share price and 100 million shares ($50B market cap). Always compare
companies by market cap, never by share price. It's also why "top-20 crypto
by market cap" is the sensible ranking — a coin priced at $0.0001 can still
be a giant if trillions of units exist.

One subtlety: market cap is the price of the *equity* only. It ignores
debt. That's fine for comparing tech companies with little debt, but for
utilities and REITs (our themes 3 and 6), which borrow heavily, analysts
often use *enterprise value* (market cap + debt − cash) instead. We'll
cover that in a future brief.

## 5. Hype check: the "AnthropicAI token" (+775%)

A token called "AnthropicAI" was reported up **775% in a day** on 1 August
([Coin Gabbar](https://www.coingabbar.com/en/crypto-currency-news/crypto-news-today-1-august-bitcoin-eth-drops-anthropic-gains)).
Sounds like a way to get in early on a hot private AI company. Here's why
that excitement probably doesn't translate into anything:

- Anthropic (the actual company) has **formally declared tokenized products
  based on its private shares invalid and unauthorized**, warning they may
  be fraudulent and hold no value, because they aren't backed by its real
  shareholder registry
  ([CryptoRank](https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/052ea-anthropic-unauthorized-stock-tokens-invalid),
  [Anthropic support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13704655-unauthorized-anthropic-stock-sales-and-investment-scams)).
- Both Anthropic and OpenAI have said pre-IPO "exposure" token structures
  violate their share-transfer rules
  ([CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/anthropic-openai-tokenized-share-warning)).
- A +775% one-day move in a tiny token is itself a red flag: thin
  liquidity means a small amount of buying (often coordinated) produces
  huge percentage gains — and equally huge losses for whoever buys the top.

**SPECULATIVE doesn't even cover it** — this is a token whose claimed link
to the underlying company is explicitly denied by that company. General
lesson: owning a token *named after* a business is not owning the business.
A claim on real profits requires actual equity.
